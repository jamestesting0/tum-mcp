# Choose and explain useful teaching

Read this after the full retained transcript/topic map and relevant course
materials. Select additional reasoning, concrete examples, limiting conditions,
corrections, links between ideas, common mistakes and explicit study advice.
Prefer a short connected explanation over a list of disconnected transcript
snippets. Remove greetings, dead time, recognizer repetition and logistics that
will not help the learner. Preserve consequential course/exam logistics only when
the professor actually states them; cite their original time.

Compare each candidate with the available slides/notes. If the slide already
states a definition, the useful note is often why it matters, the assumption it
needs or the example the professor used. Link the material with its real locator
instead of copying pages. When comparison materials are missing, say so in the
private review record and completion report; do not claim an idea is novel.

A useful section gives just enough prerequisite context, explains the critical
inference under its conditions, and retains the lecture's worked example or
contrast when that aids understanding. Define symbols, keep units/signs and
qualifiers, and distinguish a heuristic from a guarantee. Do not force this
sequence into a template, impose a word count, expand every definition or add
compulsory checks. Study/Exercises already provide broader teaching and practice.

Maintain a compact private review map, for example
`.tum-mcp/lecture-review/LECTURE_KEY.md`, with topic/time coverage, compared
material locators/hashes, source-vs-added reasoning decisions, unresolved speech
and why each selected frame is needed. This file is not browser content.
Additional explanations and diagrams should be neutral, accurate and necessary.
Keep their attribution checks here; no visible AI-added labels. Do not write
“The professor said/showed” unless the source actually supports it. A supporting
diagram uses ordinary safe content blocks, never the captured-figure class.

Names and notation are high-impact recognition failure points. Resolve them from
verified slides/materials or a carefully selected legible recording frame, not
agreement between recognizers. Keep unresolved claims out of authoritative prose.
Review all consequential claims, explicit assessment claims and cited figures.
Full audio/transcript processing is separate from full semantic review.

These writing decisions adapt two inspected sources:

- [ASD-STE100 skill writing rules](https://github.com/danyuchn/asd-ste100-skill/blob/8da6b6340aa22faf46fd07db8efbdea010cd38d9/references/writing-rules.md): clear sentence references, direct actors and stable terminology. Retain necessary technical terms and mathematical qualifications. This is not certified STE or an approved-dictionary claim.
- [LearnHarness teaching method](https://github.com/amosblomqvist/learnharness/blob/3dc88472eea41eed0d5e5f29c2f16f8d063c415b/src/agent/prompts/teacher/05-method.md) and [teaching guide](https://github.com/amosblomqvist/learnharness/blob/3dc88472eea41eed0d5e5f29c2f16f8d063c415b/src/agent/prompts/teacher/20-teaching.md): preserve prerequisites, justified inference and useful examples. Its interactive checks/learner-state machinery are not part of Lecture.

The existing Study teaching guide already adopts related principles. These are
writing adaptations, not evidence of measured learning superiority or mastery.
