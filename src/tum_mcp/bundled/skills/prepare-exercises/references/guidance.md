# Help with the next inference

Use the goal/prerequisite/inference/source worksheet in
[Study teaching](../../prepare-study/references/teaching.md) and the distinct
demands in [practice](../../prepare-study/references/practice.md).

Keep the original problem first and useful on its own. Each step's purpose names
the reasoning difficulty it addresses: choosing a premise, interpreting a symbol,
constructing an invariant or combining two results. Omit ceremonial instructions
such as “read the problem”; do not turn each algebraic manipulation into a screen.
One conceptual step is active, with compact Previous/Next arrow controls and the statement
still reachable. Full solution reveal is always available without passing steps.

An early hint points to a relevant relation or asks a smaller question. A later
hint can narrow the operation; explanation and solution may complete it. Add only
useful hint levels, without quotas. Wrong-answer feedback repairs the earliest
likely error; avoid revealing the key immediately. An optional prerequisite link
and inline reminder can repair a gap without a separate prerequisite wizard.

For deterministic answers, use the actual response form: numeric values with
explicit tolerance/units, table cells, node/edge/path selection, plotted sample
coordinates or four-option selection when recognition is the learning goal. Use a rubric/model derivation for proofs,
code/design and other open work. A rubric compares assumptions, essential reasoning
and conclusion, and admits equivalent approaches. Never use keyword matching or
pretend prose is automatically graded. Students may work on paper and compare
without typing. Do not require confidence forms, timers or self-ratings.

Review by attempting the task without looking at its solution. Then simulate one
plausible miss and request only the next hint. It should help the student do the
next inference. Check an altered case where a condition fails; merely renaming
the original task is not a transfer check. Keep list status to Not started,
In progress and Reviewed; assistance and retries are not mastery estimates.
