# Scoring rubric

Each dimension scores 1 (blocks learning), 2 (works with friction) or 3 (easy to learn
from). Score the item as a whole by its weakest place: one wrong answer makes correctness
1 even if the rest is right.

## Correctness

Facts, notation, numbers and conventions match the cited source and edition.

- 3: every number recomputed with code; every answer, explanation and option feedback agrees
  with the source; the conventions an answer depends on (tie-breaks, order, indexing) are
  stated before the task, or every valid alternative is accepted.
- 2: correct, but a convention is left implicit or a citation points at the wrong page.
- 1: a wrong fact, number, answer or trace step.

## Clarity

The student can read each passage once and know what it says.

- 3: short, direct sentences; each term is defined before it is used; one idea per
  paragraph; no line that only describes the page or tells the student to work.
- 2: a dense paragraph, an undefined term or an ambiguous instruction.
- 1: the main idea cannot be understood from the text and its pictures.

## Cognitive load

What the student has to hold in mind at once.

- 3: one new idea per section or step; the state of a procedure is shown, not tracked
  mentally; text and the picture it refers to sit together; a question fits one phone
  screen; large steps have a middle hint.
- 2: two new ideas in one step, a long question, or a trace the student has to follow
  without its state.
- 1: the student must combine several unexplained pieces before anything makes sense.

## Representation

Each idea is shown in the shape it has ([representations](../../prepare-study/references/review.md#choose-the-representation)).

- 3: procedures are traces with their state, parallel cases are tables or lists, symbols
  are formulas with each symbol explained, structures are diagrams or trees; prose says
  only what the picture cannot.
- 2: an idea that has a shape is given as prose, or a picture needs prose to be read.
- 1: the central idea of the item has no fitting picture, table, formula or code.

## Practice

Cards, questions and exercise steps make the student use the outcomes.

- 3: every outcome of the item is practised; options are plausible and each wrong
  option's feedback names its mistake; hints go from a pointer to the first operation
  to Compare; Compare and the solution answer everything that was asked.
- 2: an outcome is only recalled, a distractor is implausible, or a hint gives the answer.
- 1: practice tests something the item does not teach, or an answer is missing.

## Coverage

The item teaches what its sources say students should learn, and nothing else is assumed.

- 3: every outcome the coverage ledger assigns to the unit is taught and practised; an
  exercise answers every original part; prerequisites link to the passage that teaches
  them.
- 2: an outcome is taught but not practised, or a prerequisite is missing its link.
- 1: an assigned outcome or an original part is missing.
